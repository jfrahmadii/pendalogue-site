How the figures are calculated

Statistics Canada publishes, for Ontario as a whole, how many jobs in each industry exist because of demand from the United States, and how much each industry exports. It does not publish these for counties. This page shows how we turn the provincial figures into county figures, step by step, with a worked example, and how we check the result. The checks were run on 2026-10-14, on the data every page of this site is built from.

In three steps

  1. Start from Ontario, industry by industry. For each industry, Statistics Canada's 2022 figures for Ontario (12-10-0100): the jobs that exist because of US demand, the exports to the United States, and the exports to all countries.
  2. Share each industry among the counties by where its workers live. A county gets the part of each Ontario figure that matches its part of the industry's workers, counted by the 2021 Census where they live (98-10-0592).
  3. Add up the industries to get the county's total, and divide by the county's employed residents, or by its exports, to get a share.

Every county figure is therefore an estimate: it assumes a county's workers in an industry are as tied to US demand as that industry's workers across Ontario. Counties differ because their residents work in different industries, not because their firms differ.

Each measure

Jobs tied to US demand

For each industry:

county's jobs tied to US demand = Ontario's jobs tied to US demand in the industry × (county's residents working in the industry ÷ Ontario's residents working in it)

The county's total is the sum over its industries. Ontario's figure is Statistics Canada's "jobs due to foreign demand" for the United States: jobs in an industry that exist because of exports by all industries.

Jobs share, the ranking measure

jobs share = county's jobs tied to US demand ÷ county's employed residents, as a percent

Share of US exports that paid US duty, and the other tariff shares

These are worked out in three layers, each an average of the one below:

  1. Each product. From the US Census Bureau's records of imports from Canada: the share of the product's value that paid duty (dutiable value ÷ customs value) over 2026-06 to 2026-08; the duty itself (calculated duty ÷ customs value); and the share on tariff lines a US instrument in force lists (value on listed lines ÷ all value, 2022 imports).
  2. Each industry. The average of its products' shares, each product weighted by Ontario's 2022 exports of it to the United States. Products are matched to industries through the US Census Bureau's concordance.
  3. Each county. The average of its industries' shares, each industry weighted by the county's estimated US exports in it.

county's share = sum over its industries of (industry's share × county's US exports in the industry) ÷ county's US exports

The industry's share is Ontario-wide, at Canada-wide rates by product, so a county's share moves only with its mix of industries.

US share of exports

US share of exports = county's estimated exports to the United States ÷ its estimated exports to all countries, as a percent

Every county inherits Ontario's US share within each industry, so this too moves only with the county's mix of industries.

The industries on a county page

A county page lists the five pools of industries with the most of the county's jobs tied to US demand. Its duty share is the pool's across Ontario over 2026-06 to 2026-08, its industries weighted by their goods exports. A pool with no goods exports counted, as most services are, shows none.

A worked example: Oxford

Oxford ranks first. Its industry with the most jobs tied to US demand is motor vehicle parts.

  • Across Ontario, 48,671 jobs in motor vehicle parts exist because of US demand, and the 2021 Census counted 58,990 residents working in the industry.
  • 2,130 of them live in Oxford.
  • So Oxford gets about 1,757 of the industry's jobs tied to US demand. Census counts are rounded, so the arithmetic matches to within a few jobs.

Doing the same for every industry and adding up gives about 12,600 jobs. Divided by Oxford's employed residents, that is 22.3% of them, the highest share of any ranked county.

On duty: across Ontario, 15.9% of motor vehicle parts' US goods exports paid duty, and 97.4% of automobiles and heavy trucks' US goods exports paid duty. Averaged over all of Oxford's industries, each weighted by the county's US exports in it, 51.7% of the value of Oxford's US exports was in goods that paid duty.

The ranking

Counties are ranked on the jobs share, rounded to one decimal. Where two counties have the same share, the one with more jobs tied to US demand ranks first. Counties in a metropolitan area where the check of the method fails are not ranked.

What the figures assume

The year

These figures describe the structure of Ontario's exports in 2022, the latest year Statistics Canada publishes exports by industry for Ontario. They describe the economy before the United States tariffs of 2025 and 2026.

How a county figure is made

Ontario's exports of each industry (Statistics Canada 12-10-0100, detailed industries) are shared among counties by where that industry's workers live, counted in the 2021 Census by four-digit industry group (98-10-0592). Where the census groups are coarser than the export industries, the industries are pooled; retail and information are each pooled whole, and farms are one pool. Non-ferrous metals, whose exports are mostly refined gold, are shared by manufacturing employment instead. County figures assume each industry's workers in the county export as much per worker as that industry does across Ontario. Differences between counties come from which industries their residents work in, not from differences between firms.

Where workers are counted

Workers are counted where they live (2021 Census, place of residence), not where they work.

Estimated

Every county figure is an allocation, marked estimated; no county export figure is published by Statistics Canada.

The census week

The 2021 Census counted employment in May 2021, during Ontario's pandemic stay-at-home order, when fewer people were working than in 2022. Figures per employed resident therefore read higher than they would on 2022 employment, more so where accommodation, food and retail work was shut. No adjustment is made. Jobs tied to US demand divide a count of jobs by a count of employed residents.

Tariffs in force

The share on listed lines is the part of each industry's 2022 goods exports from Ontario to the United States (Statistics Canada, customs basis) that sits on tariff lines a US instrument in force lists, each line weighted by the United States' 2022 imports from Canada on it, applied to the industry's exports. The lines are matched to industries through the US Census Bureau's concordance of tariff lines to industries, and a line counts whole even where the tariff reaches only part of it, such as the metal content. Listed goods may still enter free under CUSMA. The share is not weighted by tariff rate; each instrument's rate is shown as its text reads.

Duties paid

The share in goods that paid US duty, and the duty paid, apply what the US Census Bureau records on imports from Canada, product by product, over 2026-06 to 2026-08, to each industry's 2022 exports: Canada-wide rates by product, not the county's own firms' CUSMA claims.

Tariffed, not yet in the duty data

Goods on lines first tariffed by an instrument that took effect after the first month of the duty data are counted separately; no rate is applied to them. The duty data covers them for only part of its months.

Peer groups

A county's peers are the counties in its urbanity class: urban, mixed or rural, set by the share of its 2021 census population living outside population centres. Size is not part of the peer key. The 2021 Census did not fully enumerate the Six Nations reserve, which lies in Brant and Haldimand-Norfolk; both keep the class Statistics Canada's counts give them, and both pages say that counting those residents could change it.

How the method is checked

No county export figure is published, so a county's own figure cannot be checked. The method can: we run the same steps for each census metropolitan area (CMA) and compare the result with the exports Statistics Canada publishes for CMAs (12-10-0138). Two tests apply. Across the CMAs, allocated and published exports must rank in nearly the same order (a rank correlation of at least the required value below). And each CMA's allocation must fall within half to twice its published figure; a county with part of its population in a CMA that fails is not ranked.

  • 2022 (gating year): rank correlation over 16 CMAs, 0.935, against a required 0.8: pass. CMAs outside half to twice the published figure: 4. Counties ranked 44, withheld 5; counties containing part of a CMA 27, outside every CMA 22. For the rural check's municipal allocation, exports carried by municipal rows with a published employment value: 319.00 of 319.11 billion dollars (0.036 percent short, from suppressed rows).
  • 2021 (reported): rank correlation over 16 CMAs, 0.944, against a required 0.8: pass. CMAs outside half to twice the published figure: 4. Counties ranked 45, withheld 4; counties containing part of a CMA 27, outside every CMA 22. For the rural check's municipal allocation, exports carried by municipal rows with a published employment value: 270.17 of 270.27 billion dollars (0.038 percent short, from suppressed rows).

Counties outside every metropolitan area

No published figure reaches a county that contains no part of a CMA. Its own figure is not checked; the method is checked on the metropolitan areas, above, and on Ontario's rural and small-town areas taken together, below.

The rural check

The share of Ontario's exports in rural and small-town areas (every municipality outside a CMA and a CA), as the method allocates it and as Statistics Canada publishes it (12-10-0180, goods exports linked to Business Register establishments), percent. The two measure different things: the published figure places exports where the exporting establishment is registered, the allocation where the industry's workers live. Shares, not dollars, are compared for that reason. No threshold is set; the check is reported, not a gate.

This check allocates by the eighteen industry groups of 98-10-0456 at municipal grain, not by the county figures' four-digit groups, which the census publishes for counties only. The allocation is shown twice: with workers where they live, as the county figures count them, and with workers moved to where they work along the 2021 Census commuting flows (98-10-0459), which is closer to how the published figure counts. The first ratio is the county figures' own method: pools of industries shared by employed residents in four-digit industry groups, rural and small town taken as Ontario less its CMAs and CAs in 98-10-0448.

The US tariffs

We read the tariffs from each US instrument's own text in the Federal Register and the Harmonized Tariff Schedule, not from summaries. Each instrument's status (in force, announced, ended) is the status on 2026-10-14. Tariff lines are matched to industries through the US Census Bureau's concordance, and each line is weighted by the United States' imports from Canada on it. Before each publication we re-read every Federal Register document published since, no more than fourteen days before.

Ontario since 2022

The panel of Ontario's exports to the United States since 2022 is Ontario-wide, from Statistics Canada's customs-basis trade tables; it is not allocated to counties.
